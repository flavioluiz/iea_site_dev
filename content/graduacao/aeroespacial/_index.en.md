## About the Program

The Aerospace Engineering program at ITA — established in 2010 — trains engineers with broad competencies to design, develop, and operate complex aerospace systems: satellites, rockets, launch vehicles, probes, and space stations. With strong integration between aeronautical and space dimensions, graduates are prepared to work across the entire aerospace sector value chain.

## Curriculum Structure

The curriculum comprises: (a) Required Courses, (b) Elective Courses, (c) Supervised Internship, and (d) Complementary Activities.

Students choose between **Option A** and **Option B**, which differ in the load of elective courses and supervised internship hours.

### Key Required Subjects

The program covers: Fluid Mechanics, Aerospace Structures (I and II), Orbital Mechanics, Aerospace Propulsion, Control Systems, Spacecraft Dynamics and Control, Computational Fluid Dynamics, Aeroelasticity, Space Systems Engineering, and Advanced Aerospace Systems Design.

### Elective Options

- **Option A:** minimum 272 class hours of electives
- **Option B:** minimum 128 class hours of electives

### Internship

- **Option A:** minimum 160 hours
- **Option B:** minimum 300 hours

## Graduate Profile

The aerospace engineer trained at ITA is qualified to:

- Design and analyze complete space systems (satellites, rockets, probes)
- Apply orbital mechanics and spacecraft dynamics
- Develop aerospace propulsion systems (solid, liquid, electric)
- Work in Guidance, Navigation and Control (GNC) of spacecraft
- Integrate space subsystems: structure, thermal, power, communications
- Lead space missions from conception to launch and operations

## Career Opportunities

- **National space sector:** AEB, INPE, DCTA/IAE
- **Defense and government:** FAB, Navy, national strategic programs
- **Private industry:** NewSpace startups, satellite operators, subsystem suppliers
- **International:** ESA, NASA, SpaceX, OneWeb, Planet Labs
- **Research and academia:** centers of excellence, technology institutes
